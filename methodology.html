How Super Evidence turns super data into evidence.

The goal is not to create another ranking table. It is to preserve the structure, source, date and meaning of Australian superannuation data well enough that a member can ask a simple question and still inspect the technical evidence underneath the answer.

1. Market coverage

Our broad market map starts with APRA's Quarterly Superannuation Product Statistics (QSPS), which lists products, investment menus and investment options reported by APRA-regulated superannuation funds. It includes public retail/industry funds as well as restricted schemes and large platform structures, so we label those distinctions rather than calling every APRA RSE an equally accessible consumer alternative.

The hierarchy is preserved:
Fund → Product → Investment Menu → Investment Option → Investment Pathway.

An investment pathway is the specific product + menu + option route through which a member accesses an investment. The same underlying option can therefore appear through several pathways. We do not count each pathway as a different investment option.

If APRA and the issuer show different values because of reporting dates or methodologies, both can be correct. We show the difference rather than overwriting one with the other.

3. Investment returns are not one universal metric

APRA publishes different return concepts depending on product structure. Super Evidence keeps these bases separate. Examples include net investment return, gross investment return net of fees, investment return net of fees, and representative-member net return after additional member-level costs.

A 10-year number is only treated as directly comparable when the time horizon, product phase and return basis are compatible.

Where one underlying option is available through multiple pathways and pathway-level investment returns differ, an option-level summary uses a conservative approach rather than selecting the most favourable pathway. Pathway records remain available underneath the summary.

4. Growth exposure is not a “risk percentage”

Growth-asset weighting describes the proportion of the strategic portfolio classified as growth under the relevant APRA methodology. It is useful for peer grouping, but it is not the same as the probability of loss or a universal risk score.

Risk can also be described using long-term volatility, issuer/APRA risk labels and the estimated frequency of negative annual returns. We keep these measures separate. We do not infer a numeric risk level from names such as “Balanced”, “Growth” or “Conservative”.

5. Fees are balance- and pathway-dependent

A single investment option can be accessed through products or menus with different administration costs. APRA therefore reports representative-member fees at specified account balances. Super Evidence uses matched representative balances for comparisons and shows a range when pathway fees differ materially.

Issuer fee calculators can additionally model disclosed fixed fees, asset-based administration fees, caps, investment costs and other rules. Insurance premiums, personal advice and member-initiated activity fees are kept separate unless explicitly included.

6. Like-for-like peer comparisons

The starting point for a peer comparison is not the marketing label. We first check product phase, diversified versus specialist structure, APRA growth band / growth exposure and performance methodology. A result can be shown as “broadly comparable” without implying the portfolios are identical.

Peer statistics are descriptive. They do not automatically become a recommendation or ranking. A higher historical return may reflect greater growth exposure, leverage or other risks.

7. History is never overwritten

Allocations, fees, objectives, names, predecessor relationships and reporting dates are versioned. Strategic target allocation, allowable ranges and actual allocation remain distinct observations. Material changes such as mergers, renames, management-style changes or large growth-allocation shifts are recorded as events rather than being erased by the latest value.

8. Research navigator, not a black-box recommendation

The site can ask what you want to understand, your selected time horizon and which option you hold so it can explain relevant facts and comparable evidence. It does not automatically use personal circumstances to choose a financial product for you.

Age can help describe the number of years to a selected retirement-planning age. It does not by itself determine an appropriate growth allocation. Personal risk tolerance, objectives, financial situation and needs can materially change a decision.

Corrections and limitations

Super Evidence is built from reported data and public disclosures, which can contain errors, revisions and timing differences. We aim to expose those issues rather than claim false precision. Before acting on a product decision, check the fund's current product disclosure and other relevant current documents.

Factual and educational information only. Not personal financial advice. Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future performance.
