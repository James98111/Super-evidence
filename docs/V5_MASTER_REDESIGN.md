# Super Evidence V5 — Master Redesign & Art Direction

## 1. Product goal
Super Evidence should feel like the leading public superannuation resource in Australia: welcoming enough for a complete beginner, useful enough for a financially confident consumer, and deep enough for an adviser or researcher.

The visual benchmark is not a comparison-table website. It is a premium financial-services product with the ease of Airbnb, the trust of a major Australian financial institution, and the information depth of Bloomberg — but only when the user asks for that depth.

Core consumer promise:

> Understand your super quickly. Compare it confidently. See the evidence when you want it.

## 2. Non-negotiable design rules
1. The first screen of every page must communicate one clear purpose.
2. A beginner must understand the page without knowing super terminology in advance.
3. No more than 4–5 headline metrics may appear before the first major scroll.
4. Every major section should lead with a takeaway, not a dataset.
5. Charts and visuals are preferred when they explain faster than prose.
6. Original issuer terminology and evidence remain accessible, but never dominate the beginner view.
7. Rankings are never the primary navigation or default frame.
8. Official fund branding is used carefully and with a clear independence disclaimer.
9. No pay-to-rank or sponsored ordering.
10. White space is functional. Do not fill empty areas simply because they exist.
11. Avoid generic AI design signals: gradients, endless rounded cards, oversized emoji-like icons, random pills, floating dashboards, decorative scribbles, overly cute illustrations, or copy written into photography.
12. Every visual component must be reusable across hundreds of funds and thousands of options.

## 3. Audience layers
### Layer A — Complete beginner
Needs:
- reassurance that they are in the right place
- plain-English definitions
- clear next actions
- visual explanations
- simple takeaways

Should not be forced to understand:
- APRA terminology
- SAA methodology
- pathway definitions
- real-return formulas
- risk-adjusted statistics
- evidence statuses

### Layer B — Financially confident consumer
Needs:
- efficient comparisons
- meaningful long-run returns
- costs in dollars
- portfolio allocation
- risk and time-horizon context
- calculators
- peer context

### Layer C — Adviser / analyst / researcher
Needs:
- source dates
- issuer evidence
- APRA cross-checks
- historic strategy changes
- benchmarks
- raw asset labels
- fee rules
- portfolio holdings
- real returns
- methodology notes
- data download / export later

The same page serves all three via progressive disclosure.

## 4. Brand direction
### Brand personality
- warm
- calm
- informed
- independent
- financially credible
- never salesy
- never patronising

### Palette
Primary navy: #102A43
Warm ivory: #FBF8F4
Terracotta accent: #B85C43
Soft terracotta: #F5E7E1
Sage: #5E7968
Pale sage: #EDF2EE
Warm stone: #F3F1ED
Muted text: #5F6D78
Divider: #DDD8D1
White: #FFFFFF

### Typography
Display / major headings: Source Serif 4 or equivalent refined editorial serif.
Body / UI / numbers: Source Sans 3 or equivalent humanist sans.

Typography rules:
- short display headlines
- avoid tiny explanatory copy
- use body text at comfortable reading size
- tabular numerals for performance and fee data
- no all-caps except small overlines/eyebrows

### Photography
Use sparingly.
- homepage: one high-quality, natural Australian lifestyle image
- major education landing pages: optional supporting photography
- fund / option / compare pages: generally no large lifestyle photography

Photography should feel candid, natural, contemporary and Australian — not corporate stock imagery.

### Iconography
Create one consistent custom line-icon family.
Required icons:
- search
- fund
- investment option
- compare
- portfolio
- shares
- property
- infrastructure
- private equity
- bonds
- cash
- fees
- return/performance
- risk
- timeframe
- calculator
- learn
- evidence/source
- verified
- history

Style:
- 1.75–2px line weight
- rounded line endings
- simple geometry
- no filled cartoon icons
- use terracotta/sage/navy sparingly

## 5. Fund logos and branding
Official fund logos should be a major visual trust cue, especially on:
- homepage fund shelf
- search results
- fund directory
- fund profile header
- comparison selector
- option breadcrumb / header

Rules:
- use official current marks where their public use is permitted
- do not recolour or distort fund marks
- retain clear whitespace around them
- provide text fallback if logo cannot be used
- include a general independence notice: fund trademarks belong to their respective owners and appearance does not imply endorsement
- fund logo use must never imply sponsorship

## 7. Homepage blueprint
### Purpose
Answer in under 5 seconds:
1. what is this site?
2. what can I do here?
3. where do I start?

### Section 1 — Hero
Left:
Eyebrow: Independent Australian super research
Headline:
> Understand your super with confidence.

Subhead:
> Explore Australia’s super funds, investment options, fees, risk and long-term performance — in plain English.

Primary search:
> Search a super fund

Search results show funds only.

Right:
one strong lifestyle image.

No ranking table, no market statistics, no dense analytics.

### Section 2 — Three ways to use the site
Three icon-led actions only:
1. Explore funds
   See each fund and the investment options it offers.
2. Compare options
   Compare returns, risk, fees and what portfolios actually invest in.
3. Use calculators
   Explore super outcomes with assumptions shown clearly.

Learn is visible in navigation, not another large homepage action.

### Section 3 — Explore Australia’s super funds
Large heading.
Short line of copy.
Grid / shelf of official fund logos.
Initial priority:
- AustralianSuper
- Australian Retirement Trust
- Aware Super
- Hostplus
- UniSuper
- Rest
- HESTA
- Cbus

Each logo is clickable only when data is loaded. Coverage-expanding funds may be visible in a muted state.

### Section 4 — One educational takeaway
One quiet feature, not a library.
Example:
> A super fund is the provider. An investment option is how your money is invested inside it.

Small illustration/icon pairing.
Link: Learn the basics →

### Section 5 — Calculator feature
Show only one calculator card:
Super projection calculator

Link to full calculator hub.

### Footer
- independence statement
- methodology
- disclaimers
- privacy
- contact later
- trademarks notice

## 8. Fund directory blueprint

- one-sentence explanation

- official logo tiles/rows

Each fund row shows only:
- logo

## 9. Fund profile blueprint
### Purpose
Answer:
> What is this fund, and what choices does it give me?

### Above the fold
- official logo
- fund name
- one short neutral description
- four concise facts maximum:
  - investment options loaded
  - default/MySuper strategy (when verified)
  - fund size / members (later)
  - fee range or product count (only if useful)

Primary actions:
- View investment options
- Compare this fund’s options

### Plain-English fund overview
One visual band explaining:
- what kind of fund it is
- default option / lifecycle structure if relevant
- notable menu structure
- whether indexed options exist

### Investment option menu
Use visual grouped cards/rows — not one dense table.
Groups:
- Diversified
- Indexed diversified
- Social / responsible
- Lifecycle
- Single asset / specialist

Each option card shows only:
- option name
- one-line description
- risk visual
- growth exposure visual
- 10-year return if verified
- investment cost
- View
- Compare

On mobile, show three facts only and reveal more on tap.

### Secondary fund information
Collapsed sections:
- Fees at fund/product level
- Insurance
- Member services
- Retirement products
- Documents
- Research & evidence

## 10. Investment option page blueprint
### Purpose
Answer:
> What is this option, what does it invest in, how risky is it, how has it performed, and what does it cost?

### Section 1 — Hero summary
- fund logo + name
- option name
- short plain-English descriptor
- Compare button

Four headline facts only:
1. Risk level
2. Growth exposure
3. 10-year return
4. Estimated annual cost at selected balance

### Section 2 — Simple takeaway
A warm tinted panel:
> In plain English

Maximum 2–3 sentences.
Example:
> This is a high-growth diversified portfolio. Most of the money is invested in assets such as shares, property and infrastructure, so it can move around more in bad markets but is designed for long-term growth.

Then 2–3 small tags:
- Long-term growth
- Diversified
- Higher ups and downs

### Section 3 — What does it invest in?
Primary visual: stacked horizontal allocation bar or compact donut.
Show broad categories with icons:
- Australian shares
- International shares
- Property
- Infrastructure
- Private equity
- Fixed income
- Cash
- Other

Under chart:
> What this means
One short sentence interpreting the mix.

Button:
View detailed allocation →

Detailed allocation shows raw issuer labels.

### Section 4 — Performance
Lead with a chart.
- 10-year long-run focus
- optional 1 / 3 / 5 / 7 / 10 year selector
- benchmark/peer context later

Simple takeaway above chart:
> Over the 10 years to [date], this option returned X% p.a. Past performance is not a forecast.

Do not show five equal-weight performance numbers before the chart.

### Section 5 — Fees
Balance selector / slider.
Large output:
> About $X per year at a $100,000 balance

Then simple breakdown chart/bar:
- admin
- investment & transaction
- reserve-funded costs where relevant

Detailed fee rule hidden.

### Section 6 — Risk & timeframe
Visual risk scale.
- low → high
- highlighted current level
- suggested timeframe
- plain-English explanation

### Section 7 — What changed over time?
Simple timeline.
Only material changes visible by default:
- rename
- merger
- major asset allocation change
- management style shift
- benchmark change

### Section 8 — Research detail
Collapsed by default.
Contains:
- source links
- observation dates
- verification status
- raw issuer allocations
- APRA classifications
- methodology assumptions
- lineage
- benchmark data
- downloadable data later

## 11. Comparison page blueprint
### Purpose
Answer:
> What is actually different between these two options?

### Builder
Fund A → option A
Fund B → option B

Use fund logos in selectors/cards.

### Section 1 — Quick takeaway
A neutral summary, not a recommendation.
Maximum 4 bullets:
- which has lower current cost
- which has higher historical long-run return
- which has higher growth exposure
- whether the comparison is genuinely like-for-like

Example:
> These are broadly comparable high-growth options.
> ART currently has the lower estimated disclosed cost.
> ART has the higher published 10-year return.
> Aware has slightly more growth exposure.

### Section 2 — Visual scorecard
Four visual comparisons:
- risk
- growth exposure
- 10-year return
- annual cost

No overall winner score.

### Section 3 — Portfolio allocation
Side-by-side stacked bars.
Broad categories first.
Small explanatory note:
> Similar labels can hide different portfolios.

### Section 4 — Performance
Line/bar chart with common horizon.
Optional real-return toggle later.

### Section 5 — Fees
Dollar comparison at the same balance.

### Section 6 — Advanced comparison
Collapsed:
- all return periods
- raw allocation labels
- historical strategy changes
- benchmarks
- evidence
- methodology

## 14. Chart system
Charts are explanatory, not decorative.

### Allocation chart
Preferred:
- stacked horizontal bar for quick composition
- category list beneath

Avoid default donut charts if labels become crowded.

### Performance chart
- common horizon
- clear annualised label
- no 3D effects
- no excessive gridlines
- start with 10 years when available

### Risk scale
- simple 5-step or 7-step horizontal scale
- visual + text label

### Fees
- dollars first
- simple stacked cost bar second
- percentages in detail

### History
- restrained vertical/horizontal timeline

## 15. Content density rules
### Homepage
Maximum body copy before first scroll: ~80 words excluding nav/search.

### Fund page
No paragraph longer than ~55 words in the consumer layer.

### Option page
No more than four headline metrics.
No more than one interpretation paragraph per section.

### Compare
Quick takeaway maximum four bullets.

### Tables
Use only for advanced/research detail or inherently tabular comparisons.

## 16. Decision-support rules
Super Evidence may interpret factual differences, e.g.:
- lower disclosed cost
- higher historical return
- more growth exposure
- broader diversification
- different portfolio mix

It must not say:
- best for you
- you should switch
- this option will outperform

Every interpretive takeaway should be tied to visible facts.

## 17. Adviser/research layer

- downloadable CSV/API
- historical returns
- historical SAA
- APRA benchmark results
- holdings
- currency exposure
- peer groups
- risk metrics
- rolling returns
- real returns
- drawdowns

This layer should live behind explicit advanced controls, not in the default consumer page.

## 18. Quality gate for every future page
Before release, each page must pass all three checks.

### Beginner test
Can a person who knows almost nothing about super explain:
- what this page is about?
- what the main takeaway is?
- what they can do next?

within 15 seconds?

### Consumer test
Can a financially confident user reach the key data within one interaction and compare options efficiently?

### Adviser test
Can a professional locate source, date, methodology and detailed values without ambiguity?

If any answer is no, redesign before release.

## 20. V5 success definition
The user should feel:

> I immediately understand what this site does.
> I can find my fund without knowing investment jargon.
> I can understand an option in under a minute.
> I can compare two options without reading a wall of numbers.
> If I want the evidence, it is all there.

The product should feel calm, warm, independent, contemporary and financially serious — never childish, loud, dense or AI-generated.
