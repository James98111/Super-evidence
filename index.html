SUPER BASICS

Super makes more sense once you know five things.

You do not need to understand investing to use this site. These are the ideas we will explain everywhere else.

1

Your fund is not your investment

Your super fund is the provider. Inside it, your money sits in an investment option such as High Growth, Balanced or an indexed option. Two people in the same fund can therefore have very different investments.

2

“High Growth” usually means more ups and downs

Growth assets such as shares are expected to grow more over long periods, but they can fall more sharply in bad markets. The percentage of growth assets is more useful than the name alone.

3

Long-term returns matter more than one good year

A single year can be unusually strong or weak. Five- and ten-year annualised returns are more useful for understanding a long-term option, while still not guaranteeing what happens next.

4

Fees should be translated into dollars

A fee percentage can feel meaningless. We convert published formulas into an estimated annual dollar amount for the balance you choose, while keeping the source and calculation visible.

5

The investment can change over time

A ten-year return may include years when an option looked different from today. We preserve strategy changes so a long history is not mistaken for the exact portfolio you own now.

PUBLIC METHODOLOGY

How Super Evidence treats data

Primary source first. Fund pages and documents are preferred for fund-specific facts.

Regulator kept separate. APRA data is used for cross-checking, history and standardisation rather than silently replacing a fund's own disclosure.

No false precision. If a source is too coarse to support a precise calculation, we do not manufacture one.

History is preserved. New values create new dated observations instead of overwriting the past.

Every displayed number can show its source. Source, date, status and calculation context remain attached to the metric.
