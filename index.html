Super basics

The five things worth understanding before you choose an investment option.

You can use this site without knowing these first. But once they make sense, almost every super comparison becomes easier.

1. Your super fund is not the same as your investment option

Your fund is the provider that holds and administers your super. Inside the fund you usually choose an investment option, such as High Growth, Balanced or Indexed. The investment option largely determines how your money is invested and how much risk you take.

2. “High Growth” usually means more ups and downs

Growth assets such as shares, property, infrastructure and private equity are generally expected to deliver stronger long-term growth, but their value can fall sharply in bad markets. A higher growth percentage does not automatically mean a better option.

3. A 10-year return is usually more useful than a 1-year return

One year can be dominated by a market boom or fall. Longer periods give you more information about how an option has behaved across different conditions. But you should also check whether the investment strategy changed during those 10 years.

4. Small fee differences compound

Fees reduce the amount left invested for you. The difference between two fee percentages can look tiny, so our fee calculator converts them into dollars at your actual balance.

5. Past performance is useful evidence — not a promise

Historical returns tell you what happened, not what will happen. They are most useful when considered alongside risk, fees, portfolio composition and whether the strategy itself has remained comparable.
